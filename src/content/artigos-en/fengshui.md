Feng Shui is an ancient Chinese philosophy that arranges spaces to support wellbeing and the flow of energy. It is usually applied to physical places, but its principles also work in UX design, for building digital interfaces that are balanced, intuitive and pleasant to use.

In this article, I look at how Feng Shui concepts can improve usability and the user experience in digital products.

## What is Feng Shui and how does it connect to UX design?

Feng Shui means "wind and water". Its goal is harmony through the organization of space, the flow of energy (chi) and the balance of the elements. In UX design we deal with visual organization, information architecture and smooth navigation, principles that relate directly to this philosophy.

If a cluttered room causes confusion and discomfort, so does a poorly structured interface. Applying Feng Shui concepts can make products feel more natural and work better.

## Feng Shui principles applied to UX

### 1. Flow and intuitive navigation

In Feng Shui, energy should flow freely and naturally. In UX, that means clear navigation without friction. Some practices:

- Build well-defined visual hierarchies.
- Avoid confusing or overly long paths.
- Make sure users always know where they are and where they can go.

### 2. Balance and visual harmony

Feng Shui balances the five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal and water) to create pleasant spaces. In design, we can create harmony through:

- Carefully planned color palettes.
- Legible typography with a clear hierarchy.
- Balanced use of white space to avoid visual clutter.

### 3. Strategic placement of elements

Just as furniture should be placed to improve the flow of energy in a room, interface elements should be arranged to make interaction and accessibility easier. This includes:

- Placing important elements (buttons, menus) where users expect to find them.
- Following reading and scanning patterns (for example, the F and Z patterns).
- Building layouts that guide the user's eyes naturally.

### 4. Minimalism and simplicity

In Feng Shui, too many objects create stagnant energy. In UX, too much information creates confusion. So:

- Remove unnecessary elements.
- Prefer a clean, direct design.
- Use white space to make the interface lighter and more organized.

### 5. Senses and emotions

Feng Shui considers how a space makes a person feel. In UX, the emotional side matters a great deal. To improve it:

- Choose colors that evoke the right emotions (blue for calm, red for urgency, green for wellbeing).
- Use subtle animations to make interactions smoother.
- Invest in microinteractions that give the user immediate feedback.

## Conclusion

Feng Shui and UX design share a goal: spaces that feel harmonious and work well. By applying principles of flow, balance, organization and minimalism, we can design interfaces that are easier to navigate and also leave the user feeling at ease.

When you design your next interface, ask yourself: "Does this design flow naturally? Does it feel harmonious?" If the answer is yes, you may be applying Feng Shui without even noticing.
