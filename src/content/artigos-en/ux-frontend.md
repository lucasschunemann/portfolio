Changing careers is never easy, especially when moving between two fields that can look quite different at first. In my case, going from frontend development to UX design meant learning all the time, with plenty of challenges and discoveries along the way. Today I can see how my earlier experience shaped the way I think about design and helped me build better digital products.

## From developer to designer: what made me switch?

Working in frontend, I was always interested in how people interacted with the digital products I helped build. While implementing layouts and features, I started to notice that some visual and structural choices directly affected usability. I kept asking myself: "Why do some designs work so well while others feel frustrating?" That is how I found UX design.

I wanted to design interfaces with the user at the center, not only build them, and that led me to study UX further. Little by little I explored topics such as user research, wireframing, usability testing and design thinking.

## What frontend taught me about UX

My technical background as a developer gave me several advantages in this transition:

- **Logical thinking and structure**: in frontend, every element has a specific role in the code. That helped me structure more coherent and functional navigation flows in UX.
- **Understanding technical limits**: knowing what is feasible in development lets me create more realistic designs that can be built without major headaches.
- **Working with developers**: as a former dev, I can communicate well with engineering teams and translate between what designers and programmers need.
- **Components and design systems**: coming from frontend, the idea of design systems always made sense to me. It helped me build solutions that scale and stay consistent.

## The challenges of the transition

Not everything was easy. Moving into UX required a new set of skills, especially in user research and visual design. At first I tended to focus more on technical feasibility than on the user's experience. Learning to balance those two worlds was essential.

Another challenge was positioning myself in the market. Since my background and experience were more technical, I needed to build a strong portfolio to show my strategic thinking and UX skills. Working on my own projects and studying real cases helped a lot at that stage.

## Conclusion: was it worth it?

Absolutely. UX design lets me work on what really interests me: building products that matter to the people who use them. Moving from frontend to UX made me see digital products in a broader, more connected way.

If you are thinking about making this transition, my advice is: dive into learning, build a hands-on portfolio and use your technical experience to your advantage. The market values professionals who bring together technology and design.
